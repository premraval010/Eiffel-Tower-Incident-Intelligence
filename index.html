01 · Dashboard

The core question remains unsettled but the media has already ruled against BAPS.

SETE, the tower operator, confirmed it accepted the conditions and said it was wrong to. That makes the staffing decision the employer's. The question is no longer whether this happened. It is what exactly was asked, and who is responsible for how it was delivered. Although, BAPS apologised on the same day of the incident.

What happened
A BAPS delegation of about 100 visited on Saturday 5 September, tied to the temple consecration the next day. Female staff, including subcontractors, were told to leave posts or avoid certain zones. In some posts men replaced them.
Reuters via CBSfranceinfoCNN
Confirmed
SETE stated BAPS asked for a visit limiting interactions with women, and that these conditions should not have been accepted. An admission against its own interest, which makes it strong evidence. Staff struck on 7 September. Two investigations are open.
SETE via France 24franceinfoPublic Sénat
Disputed
The exact wording. Written or verbal. Who asked, who approved. Whether BAPS requested removal, or requested reduced interaction and SETE chose removal as the method.
What changed since 9 Sep
India's MEA formally distanced itself on 8 September, calling the tower issue “a matter purely between the entities concerned”. The National Assembly President joined the French criticism. The story then split in two: France 24 reported that it unites French politicians but divides the Indian diaspora, and a backlash-against-Indians angle opened. Gujarati-language OpIndia published a critical opinion, and its English edition framed the row as a workplace-equality question rather than defending the arrangement.
Biggest exposure
The BAPS statement is non-responsive. "At no time was access denied to anyone" addresses visitor access. The allegation is about employees being moved. Journalists have noticed.
BAPS statement via OrganiserCNN
Where the evidence currently sits
Accommodation, badly executedBAPS asked for reduced interaction. SETE chose removal.
Request to exclude womenBAPS asked for a workplace arranged around employees' gender.

Analyst position, not a finding. It sits right of centre because SETE's own wording refers to women, and because no BAPS document exists to support the milder reading. A written request with no reference to sex would move this marker left.

How these bands were assigned
  • Reputational, High. Scored on reach, durability of frame, and whether the core allegation is confirmed. All three point high. Not Critical, because SETE absorbs part of the blame.
  • Legal, Medium. French discrimination law places the workplace duty on the employer. BAPS is the customer. No complaint against BAPS reported.
  • Communications, High. Scored on whether the organisation's own position survives scrutiny. It does not.
  • Escalation, Medium-High. Two investigations, one works-council demand for a written framework, active political environment.

Not scored: financial risk, not assessable from public information.

05 · Central Question

Did BAPS ask for women to be removed, or ask for something SETE delivered that way?

This decides whether the correct description is a badly executed accommodation or a request to exclude women.

Was a request made?
Yes
Confirmed by SETE. Not denied by BAPS.
Did it reference women?
Probably
SETE's wording says so. BAPS denies. No document.
Written or verbal?
Unknown
Nothing public. Investigations will likely establish it.
Did SETE accept?
Yes
Admitted. Approver not named.
Were women moved?
Yes
Consistent across staff, works council and CGT.
Did BAPS foresee it?
Unknown
No evidence either way. Most consequential open question.
A request to limit interaction and a request to remove women are not the same act. But at a staffed monument, the first can only be delivered by doing the second.
Distinction 1
Religious boundary vs workplace discrimination

A vow constrains the monk. It becomes something else when observance is delivered by changing another person's working conditions based on their gender. French law treats that transfer point as the operative fact.

AssessmentThe vow is not misogyny. The implementation was still discrimination, because staff experienced different treatment based on gender.
Distinction 2
Accommodation vs exclusion

An accommodation adjusts the practitioner. Routing monks through low-staffed areas, or visiting outside hours, would not have caused this. An exclusion adjusts other people.

AssessmentWhat was delivered was an exclusion. Whether BAPS asked for an accommodation and got one is unproven.
Distinction 3
Intent vs effect

The BAPS position is an intent argument. French law and French opinion are effect-based. The staff statement is entirely about effect: sidelined, replaced, made invisible.

AssessmentAn intent defence does not engage an effect-based accusation. This is why the statement did not reduce pressure.
Distinction 4
Who carries responsibility

The workplace duty sits with the employer, which is why the investigations target SETE's internal approval. The penal code also covers conditioning a service on a protected characteristic (art. 225-1 to 225-4), but no such case has been reported.

AssessmentLegal responsibility most likely sits with SETE. Reputational responsibility is being assigned to both. Winning the legal point would not fix the reputational one.

10 · Diaspora & Backlash

The story has stopped being about one morning

Since 9 September the controversy has widened along three tracks that have little to do with the Eiffel Tower. Each carries a different risk, and two of them are risks to Indians in France generally, not to BAPS alone.

Track 1
Diaspora split
Indian groups in France are publicly divided. One side calls BAPS unrepresentative of Hinduism. The other is producing first-person defences at volume.
Track 2
Racism risk
The French far right has taken up the story. Indian organisations that criticise BAPS have simultaneously warned the affair must not become a pretext for racism.
Track 3
Record revisited
Coverage has moved to the New Jersey labour case and to claims about how the Paris land was allocated. Older material, new audience.
Track 1 · The diaspora is publicly split, and both sides are loud

France 24 framed it directly: the row unites French politicians but divides the Indian diaspora. On one side, Indian Alliance Paris and Hindus for Human Rights, both of which position their criticism as coming from within Hindu and Indian communities rather than outside them. On the other, a large and growing volume of first-person posts from BAPS women, several passing a thousand likes.

Why this is different from ordinary criticism. A critique voiced by Hindu organisations cannot be answered with a claim of anti-Hindu prejudice. That response, which is available against a French union or a French politician, is not available here. Reaching for it anyway would be the single most damaging move BAPS could make this week.

What it means practically. The community response is working on its own terms and should continue. It should not be pointed at the diaspora critics. A public argument between Indian groups in Paris is a better story than the original incident, and it would run longer.

Track 2 · Racism and the far right, the risk nobody controls

National Herald reported fears of increased racism and anti-immigrant sentiment following the affair. French far-right figures have alleged that land was allocated to BAPS under Indian government pressure, linking it to defence trade. No evidence for that claim has been published, and this brief treats it as unsupported.

The important detail is that BAPS’s sharpest diaspora critics issued the racism warning themselves. Indian Alliance Paris said the affair must not serve as a pretext for more racism in the same statement in which it called BAPS a retrograde sect. They are arguing against BAPS and for Indians in France at once.

Assessment. This is the track where BAPS has least control and most to lose reputationally without having done anything further. It is also the track where a badly judged statement does the most harm, because anything that reads as Hindu grievance politics will be absorbed into the far-right frame rather than countering it.

Track 3 · The record is being revisited

France 24 published a long feature on 10 September following a former BAPS temple worker in the United States, reviving the Robbinsville allegations. The factual position, which the brief states plainly: the US federal investigation closed in 2025 without criminal charges, and the civil class action continues. Both halves belong in any account of it.

Outlook and Newslaundry have run explainer pieces connecting the Eiffel Tower incident to BAPS’s wider record and its relationship with Indian political leadership.

Assessment. This is the ordinary mechanics of a news cycle. It is also why the incident matters more than its size suggests: it functions as a key that opens older files for a new audience.

What the critical side talks about

Almost every high-frequency critical term is about effect on a third party: discrimination, humiliated, sidelined, invisible, removed, workplace. The words describe what happened to someone.

A second cluster is about place and propriety rather than the vow: public monument, why visit at all, égalité, laws of the Republic. This is the argument BAPS has least prepared for, and it is rising.

What the supportive side talks about

Almost every high-frequency supportive term is about internal meaning and intent: brahmacharya, discipline, vow, context, lived experience, maryada. The words describe what something means.

Only two supportive terms engage the other side’s frame at all, “not discrimination” and “misunderstanding”, and both are denials rather than explanations.

The clearest diagnostic in the brief. An intent vocabulary cannot answer an effect accusation. Until a supportive message uses the other side’s words, the two conversations run in parallel and neither moves.

Method, stated plainly. Analyst-coded term frequencies across roughly 60 items read for this brief: news articles in four languages, named X posts, Instagram posts and their comment threads. Not a computed scrape of the full social corpus, and no platform API was used. Treat the bands as directional, and describe it externally as a reading of the reviewed material rather than a measurement of public opinion.

12 · Contradictions & Risk

Where the accounts diverge, and what would settle them

1 · Was the request about gender?

SETE: the delegation asked for a visit limiting interactions with women. BAPS: never rooted in gender, it was about delegation size.

Better supported: SETE. Its statement damages SETE, which is why it carries weight. If the concern were size, the instruction would have applied to all staff.

Resolved by: the original request in writing, or testimony from the SETE employee who received it.

2 · Access vs displacement

BAPS: no one was denied access. Staff: women were moved off posts and replaced by men.

Better supported: both may be true. They answer different questions. That is the problem, because BAPS answered the easier one.

Resolved by: nothing factual. This is a framing failure.

3 · Internal BAPS messaging

A volunteer told AFP that saints have rules, which is why men and women are separated. BAPS Paris later told CNN the request was never rooted in gender. Not a factual contradiction, since the first concerned ceremonies, but a coherence failure that journalists are already pairing. It also shows unbriefed volunteers speaking to a global wire agency.

4 · Was the monument privatised?

BAPS: normal opening hours. Some commentary: the tower was privatised. No union or SETE statement in the reviewed record describes a closure to the public. This is one of the few places a BAPS clarification would be factually solid.
